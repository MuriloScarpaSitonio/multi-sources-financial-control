Why VWRA starts behind

Every monthly transfer pays contribution IOF, the BRL→USD spread and the greater of US$2 or 0.20% to buy VWRA. Repatriation pays the USD→BRL spread and withdrawal IOF.

Why VWRA can catch up

The model uses a 0.14% annual fee for VWRA versus approximately 0.44% look-through for VWRA11.

Your existing USD is different

Money already abroad avoids a new contribution IOF and BRL→USD conversion. A VWRA purchase still pays the bank's trading fee.

How monthly contributions change the answer
  • Every contribution has its own holding period. After 18 years, the oldest contribution is 18 years old and the newest is less than one month old.
  • Recent contributions keep adding fresh IOF and FX drag, pushing the whole-portfolio break-even later than one isolated contribution.
  • If contributions stop, the remaining portfolio continues aging and direct VWRA catches up faster.
  • The editable amount changes the result slightly because the fixed R$0.98 charge is proportionally larger for small transfers.

Model assumptions and sources
  • The editable amount is invested at the start of every month.
  • 7% gross annual return; editable defaults of 0.75% spread in both directions, 1.10% contribution IOF and 0.38% withdrawal IOF; R$0.98 fixed charge per personal conversion.
  • Each VWRA purchase pays the greater of US$2 or 0.20%. The US$2 minimum is converted using USD/BRL 5.14.
  • VWRA annual fee 0.14%; VWRA11 estimated look-through fee 0.44%.
  • No bid/ask spread, tracking difference or final Brazilian income tax. Returns are deterministic to isolate costs; they are not a forecast.
