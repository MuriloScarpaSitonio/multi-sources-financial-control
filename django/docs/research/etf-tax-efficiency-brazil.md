# ETF tax efficiency for a Brazilian tax resident: VT, VWRA, WRLD11, VWRA11

Date researched: 2026-08-24  
Scope: Brazilian tax resident individual, long horizon / FIRE, comparing:

- `VT`: Vanguard Total World Stock ETF, U.S.-domiciled ETF.
- `VWRA`: Vanguard FTSE All-World UCITS ETF (USD) Accumulating, Ireland-domiciled UCITS ETF, London Stock Exchange USD ticker `VWRA`.
- `WRLD11`: Investo FTSE Global Equities ETF Fundo de Indice - Investimento no Exterior, B3-listed Brazilian ETF investing in `VT`.
- `VWRA11`: Investo FTSE All-World Fundo de Indice, B3-listed Brazilian ETF investing in `VWRA`.

This is a research note, not tax/legal/investment advice. Tax reporting can turn on facts not covered here, such as acquisition dates, broker documentation, whether assets are held through an entity, succession planning, and future regulatory changes.

## Executive view

For a Brazilian individual investing for decades, the main trade-off is not just expense ratio. It is a combination of dividend withholding leakage, Brazilian tax timing, U.S. estate tax exposure, local reporting effort, and fund wrapper cost.

My read of the primary sources is:

1. **Direct `VT` is the lowest headline fund fee but the weakest tax wrapper for a Brazilian resident.** Vanguard lists `VT` at a 0.06% expense ratio, but it distributes income and is a U.S.-domiciled RIC security. A nonresident alien generally faces 30% U.S. withholding on U.S.-source dividends absent a treaty reduction, and Brazil is not listed in the IRS A-Z income tax treaty list. The IRS also treats U.S.-organized corporate stock as U.S.-situated property for nonresident-not-citizen estate tax; Form 706-NA filing is triggered when aggregate U.S.-situated assets exceed USD 60,000. Sources: Vanguard `VT` page, IRS Pub. 515, IRS treaty list, IRS nonresident estate tax page and Form 706-NA instructions.
2. **Direct `VWRA` is the cleanest foreign-domiciled ETF structure on taxes/estate among the two direct foreign options.** Vanguard identifies the share class as Irish UCITS, Vanguard Funds plc, Ireland domicile, USD accumulating, ISIN `IE00BK5BQT80`, and says all dividends are reinvested for accumulation shares. Vanguard also reduced the OCF for the FTSE All-World UCITS ETF unhedged ETF class from 0.19% to 0.14% effective 2026-07-28. Since `VWRA` is stock/shares in an Irish company rather than a U.S.-organized corporation, it should generally avoid direct U.S.-situs estate-tax exposure under the IRS stock situs rule. The U.S. dividend withholding happens inside the Irish fund and is an embedded drag, not a dividend received by the Brazilian individual.
3. **`WRLD11` is operationally simple but pays for the convenience and retains the U.S. dividend layer.** Investo's WRLD11 factsheet says it targets `VT`, has a Brazil fee of 0.30% plus exterior fee of 0.06%, total 0.36% p.a., and is subject to 15% Brazilian income tax on capital gain. The wrapper avoids direct U.S.-situs ownership by the individual, but the underlying `VT` still bears U.S. dividend withholding before value reaches the Brazilian ETF.
4. **`VWRA11` is structurally the most convenient way to get the Ireland UCITS wrapper on B3, but its exact all-in fee needs monitoring.** Investo's VWRA11 factsheet says the Brazilian fund targets `VWRA`, has 0.30% local fee plus 0.19% exterior fee, total 0.49% p.a. However, Vanguard's own shareholder notice and factsheet say the `VWRA` OCF is 0.14% effective 2026-07-28. Economically, if the local 0.30% remains unchanged and the underlying OCF is passed through at 0.14%, the current all-in drag would be 0.44% p.a.; Investo's latest scraped factsheet still publishes 0.49%. Treat 0.49% as the currently published Investo total and 0.44% as the implied current look-through cost until Investo updates or confirms.

The tax-efficient short list for new, decades-long money is therefore **direct `VWRA` versus `VWRA11`**, not `VT` versus `WRLD11`. Direct `VWRA` has the lower annual fee and much deeper underlying market; `VWRA11` eliminates personal remittances, foreign-broker/DAA transaction work and direct foreign succession administration. Whether the one-time FX/IOF cost justifies direct `VWRA` depends heavily on whether the remittance is correctly classified as an investment transfer (1.10% IOF) or merely availability abroad (3.5%).

## Brazilian taxation after Lei 14.754/2023: direct foreign ETFs

Lei 14.754/2023 requires a Brazilian resident individual to declare income from capital invested abroad separately in the annual income tax return (`DAA`) for foreign financial investments and profits/dividends of controlled foreign entities; the rate is 15%, with no deduction from the tax base. The law expressly includes foreign investment fund quotas, fixed and variable income securities, derivatives and equity interests in the definition of foreign financial investments. Source: Lei 14.754/2023, arts. 2 and 3, Planalto: https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2023/lei/l14754.htm

Receita's IN RFB 2180/2024 implements the regime. It defines "rendimentos" broadly to include currency variation versus BRL, interest, premiums, discounts, profit participations, dividends, and gains in secondary-market sales, including sale gains on non-controlled foreign shares. For foreign financial investments, the 15% tax is computed in the `DAA` in the calendar year in which income is effectively perceived, under the cash regime. Acquisition cost and disposal proceeds are translated to BRL at the applicable Banco Central closing sell rate on their respective event dates; therefore BRL depreciation against the investment currency is part of taxable gain. The 2026 Receita Q&A expressly says there is no small-sale exemption for these post-2023 foreign-investment gains. Sources: IN RFB 2180/2024, arts. 8-10: https://normas.receita.fazenda.gov.br/sijut2consulta/link.action?idAto=136603 and Receita's 2026 IRPF Q&A: https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/perguntas-e-respostas/dirpf/p-r-irpf-2026-v1-00-2026-04-23.pdf

Losses realized in foreign financial investments may be offset against foreign financial investment income in the same period; excess losses may offset profits/dividends from foreign controlled entities in the same period; remaining accumulated losses may be carried forward against the IN's art. 2 income in later periods. Losses are offset once, at nominal value, without monetary adjustment. Source: IN RFB 2180/2024, art. 11: https://normas.receita.fazenda.gov.br/sijut2consulta/link.action?idAto=136603

Foreign income tax paid may be deducted from Brazilian IRPF only where an income tax treaty permits credit or where there is reciprocity; the deduction is limited, is not allowed for tax refundable/creditable abroad, cannot be carried to prior/future years, and the foreign tax paid on one foreign financial investment cannot offset Brazilian tax on another investment. Receita's manual also says proof of reciprocity is not required for the United States, Germany and the United Kingdom, and reciprocity does not cover U.S. state or municipal taxes. Sources: Lei 14.754/2023, art. 4; IN RFB 2180/2024, art. 12; Receita manual "Eventos do Patrimonio" and "Rendimentos Recebidos do Exterior": https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/meu-imposto-de-renda/preenchimento/manual-mir/patrimonio/eventos-do-patrimonio and https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/meu-imposto-de-renda/pagamento/carne-leao/rendimentos

Reporting/timing differs materially:

- Direct `VT`/`VWRA`: retain lot-level historic BRL acquisition costs and documentation; report the asset and its annual realized income/loss through the DAA's foreign-property events. Tax is settled through the annual DAA, not a monthly DARF. Receita's 2026 filing guide is explicit about historic transaction-date conversion and the "Rendimento ou Perda" event: https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/perguntas-e-respostas/dirpf/p-r-irpf-2026-v1-00-2026-04-23.pdf
- `WRLD11`/`VWRA11`: report Brazilian exchange trades monthly, pay the DARF by the last business day of the following month, and carry results into the DAA's variable-income schedule.
- A separate Banco Central CBE filing applies only when total foreign assets reach USD 1 million at 31 December (USD 100 million for quarterly CBE). The current USD 60,000 U.S. estate threshold is unrelated to CBE. Source: Banco Central: https://www.bcb.gov.br/estabilidadefinanceira/cbe

Implication for direct `VT`: `VT` distributes income. Vanguard says `VT` has a quarterly distribution schedule. U.S. withholding on that distribution is generally 30% for foreign persons receiving U.S.-source income, absent treaty reduction. Brazil is not on the IRS A-Z income tax treaty country list, so no Brazil-U.S. treaty rate reduction is available. For Brazilian IRPF, the U.S. federal tax withheld on the `VT` dividend should generally be creditable up to the Brazilian tax attributable to that same income because Receita recognizes U.S. reciprocity without proof; however, the U.S. 30% withholding exceeds the Brazilian 15% rate, so the excess is a permanent drag under IN RFB 2180's no-carryover/no-cross-investment rule. Sources: Vanguard `VT` page: https://advisors.vanguard.com/investments/products/vt/vanguard-total-world-stock-etf; IRS Pub. 515: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p515; IRS treaty list: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z; Receita sources above.

Implication for direct `VWRA`: Vanguard identifies `VWRA` as an accumulating Irish UCITS share class and states that all dividends are reinvested for accumulation shares. Because Brazilian law taxes foreign financial investment income when effectively perceived by the individual, the individual generally has no dividend cash event from `VWRA`; economic dividends are reflected in NAV and taxed in Brazil when the investor sells/redeems with gain. U.S. withholding suffered by the Irish fund on U.S. dividends is embedded in fund performance and is not a tax paid by the Brazilian individual on income received by the individual; I would not treat it as a personal foreign tax credit absent specific documentation and advice. Sources: Vanguard `VWRA` product page and factsheet: https://www.vanguard.co.uk/professional/product/etf/equity/9679/ftse-all-world-ucits-etf-usd-accumulating and https://fund-docs.vanguard.com/FTSE_All-World_UCITS_ETF_USD_Accumulating_9679_EU_INT_UK_EN.pdf; Lei 14.754/2023 and IN RFB 2180/2024 sources above.

## Brazilian taxation: B3-listed Brazilian ETFs

Receita classifies exchange-traded fund quotas, including stock-index ETF quotas traded on exchanges or organized OTC markets, as Brazilian variable income. Source: Receita "Renda Variavel": https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/meu-imposto-de-renda/pagamento/renda-variavel

For exchange-traded variable income, Receita states that net gains in Brazil are generally taxed at 15% for common trades and 20% for day trades. Receita's exemption page says the monthly BRL 20,000 exemption for listed stock sales does **not** apply to trades in stock-index ETF quotas. Sources: Receita "Bolsa de Valores" and "Isencoes": https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/meu-imposto-de-renda/pagamento/renda-variavel/bolsa-de-valores-1/bolsa-de-valores and https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/meu-imposto-de-renda/pagamento/renda-variavel/bolsa-de-valores-1/isencoes

For practical purposes, `WRLD11` and `VWRA11` are taxed to the Brazilian individual as B3 ETF quotas: no annual Brazilian tax while merely holding, and tax on realized net gain on sale (15% common trade / 20% day trade). Ordinary exchange sales have 0.005% IR withheld at source as a credit/advance against the investor's calculated liability. They are reported and paid in the Brazilian variable-income workflow, not under the Lei 14.754 direct-foreign-investment workflow. Their underlying funds' foreign dividend withholding is reflected inside the local fund's NAV, not paid directly by the individual. Source for the current ETF rates and withholding mechanics: Receita's 2026 IRPF Q&A, questions 706-711: https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/perguntas-e-respostas/dirpf/p-r-irpf-2026-v1-00-2026-04-23.pdf

Ordinary-trade B3 losses can offset ordinary-trade B3 gains in the same or later months; day-trade losses remain in a separate day-trade basket. This domestic exchange loss basket is separate from the annual foreign-financial-investment basket, so a loss in `WRLD11`/`VWRA11` cannot offset a gain or dividend from direct `VT`/`VWRA`, and vice versa. Source: Receita's current compensation guidance: https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/meu-imposto-de-renda/pagamento/renda-variavel/bolsa-de-valores-1/compensacoes

Investo also states in its ETF FAQ that Brazilian equity ETFs are taxed at 15% on sale capital gains, or 20% for day trade, with DARF paid by the investor by the last business day of the following month, and that Brazilian ETFs do not distribute dividends directly to investors; dividends received by the ETF are automatically reinvested. This is manager-provided material rather than Receita law, but it is consistent with Receita's variable-income pages. Source: Investo WRLD11 page FAQ: https://www.investoetf.com/etf/wrld11/

## U.S. withholding and estate tax

The IRS says most U.S.-source income received by a foreign person is subject to 30% U.S. tax unless reduced by treaty, and that dividends paid by U.S. corporations to foreign payees are generally subject to Chapter 3 withholding. Source: IRS Pub. 515: https://www.irs.gov/publications/p515

The IRS A-Z income tax treaty list does not include Brazil. Source: IRS treaty list: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z

For estate tax, the IRS says nonresidents who are not U.S. citizens are subject to U.S. estate tax on U.S.-situated assets. U.S.-situated assets include stock of corporations organized in or under U.S. law, even if held through a nominee; Form 706-NA is required if U.S.-situated assets exceed USD 60,000 at death. The Form 706-NA instructions state that stock of corporations organized under U.S. law is U.S.-located property, while other corporate stock is located outside the United States. Sources: IRS nonresident estate tax page and Form 706-NA instructions: https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/some-nonresidents-with-us-assets-must-file-estate-tax-returns and https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706na

Implications:

- Direct `VT`: U.S.-situs asset for estate tax because it is a U.S.-domiciled Vanguard ETF/RIC share. The threshold applies to the aggregate gross value of the decedent's U.S.-situated assets, not to each security or brokerage account. USD 60,000 is both the Form 706-NA filing threshold and, via the USD 13,000 unified credit for a non-treaty estate, approximately the practical exemption-equivalent; it should not be read as a USD 60,000 deduction from a larger estate.
- Direct `VWRA`: Irish corporate/fund share, generally non-U.S.-situs under the IRS stock situs rule.
- `WRLD11` / `VWRA11`: Brazilian fund quotas held by the individual, not direct U.S. stock for the individual. The Brazilian funds may hold foreign ETFs internally, but the investor's owned security is the Brazilian fund quota.

Vanguard's SEC-filed materials show `VT` is ETF shares of a Delaware-organized U.S. registered investment company; this, combined with the IRS situs rule, is the basis for treating it as U.S.-situs. Sources: SEC/Vanguard filing: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/857489/000119312526077566/0001193125-26-077566-index.htm and Vanguard statement of additional information: https://workplace.vanguard.com/assets/corp/fund_communications/pdf_publish/us-products/prospectuses/sai023.pdf

The same aggregation concern applies to directly held shares of U.S.-organized companies, not only `VT`; the brokerage's country and the use of a nominee do not change the IRS situs rule.

## Brazilian succession / ITCMD

Lei Complementar 227/2026 now supplies national ITCMD rules. It includes investment-fund quotas and shares as taxable property, uses market value for financial investments at death, requires progressive state rates within the Senate cap, and assigns movable property/securities to the state of the Brazilian-domiciled decedent regardless of where the asset is held. Accordingly, moving from a B3 ETF to `VT` or `VWRA` does **not** by itself avoid Brazilian ITCMD. Exact brackets, exemptions, procedure and constitutional timing still depend on the investor's state law. Sources: LC 227/2026, arts. 146-159 and 182: https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/lcp/lcp227.htm and Senate Resolution 9/1992 (maximum 8%): https://legis.senado.leg.br/norma/590017/publicacao?tipoDocumento=RSF&tipoTexto=PUB

The key product distinction is therefore administrative and potentially additive foreign tax: a B3 quota is locally administered; direct `VWRA` is a foreign asset but generally outside U.S. estate situs; direct `VT` can face U.S. estate filing/tax in addition to Brazilian ITCMD. LC 227 does not itself provide an obvious credit for foreign estate tax, so any dual-tax case needs state-specific succession advice.

## IOF and personal FX friction

The currently compiled IOF regulation distinguishes an ordinary transfer that places funds at a Brazilian resident's disposal abroad from one made specifically for investment. Article 15-B, XXI sets 3.5% for the former, while XXI-A sets 1.10% for a transfer abroad "com finalidade de investimento." A bank or remittance provider may therefore quote 3.5% when its transaction is coded as availability in an overseas account even though a properly documented brokerage-investment remittance can fall under the 1.10% category. Residual inbound exchange operations are 0.38% under art. 15-B, XXV. Source: Decreto 6.306/2007, art. 15-B, XXI, XXI-A and XXV (compiled text): https://planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2007-2010/2007/decreto/d6306compilado.htm

For a provider charging a 0.99% FX spread in each direction, the approximate direct-investment friction is:

| Outward IOF classification | Approx. entry friction | Approx. round-trip friction, including 0.38% inbound IOF | Approximate holding-period break-even: direct `VWRA` vs `VWRA11` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Investment, 1.10% IOF | about 2.1% | about 3.5% | about 10 years using Investo's published 0.49%; about 12 years using implied 0.44% |
| Availability, 3.5% IOF | about 4.5% | about 5.9% | about 17 years using Investo's published 0.49%; about 20 years using implied 0.44% |

The break-even calculation compares `VWRA`'s current 0.14% OCF with either Investo's published 0.49% total or the 0.44% look-through cost implied by Vanguard's fee cut. It treats the stated spreads and IOF rates approximately/additively and ignores brokerage, bid/ask spreads, tracking difference, the timing of contributions and tax-base nuances. Confirm whether the bank's inbound quote already absorbs the statutory 0.38% and ask whether the outbound transfer can be documented/coded as investment. The fixed BRL 0.98 fee is immaterial except for very small conversions.

## Dividend withholding layers

The rough dividend leakage picture is:

- Direct `VT`: U.S. and non-U.S. companies pay dividends into a U.S. RIC; the fund distributes to the Brazilian investor. U.S.-source dividends paid out of the U.S. fund to the Brazilian nonresident alien are generally withheld at 30% because there is no U.S.-Brazil income tax treaty rate. Brazilian 15% tax applies to the dividend income under Lei 14.754, but the U.S. federal withholding should usually credit up to the Brazilian tax on that same dividend. Since 30% exceeds 15%, there is normally no extra Brazil tax on those U.S.-withheld dividends but an excess U.S. tax drag remains.
- Direct `VWRA`: U.S. dividends paid to the Irish UCITS are generally subject to 15% U.S. withholding under the U.S.-Ireland treaty layer. IRS treaty material explains that treaty rates can reduce U.S. withholding, and the official Ireland Revenue treaty-rate table lists United States-source dividends to Ireland at 5%/15%; portfolio dividends are generally the 15% case. The investor does not receive cash dividends because the share class accumulates; Brazil taxes realized gains when the investor disposes of the shares. Non-U.S. source dividend withholding varies by source market and is embedded in fund returns. Sources: IRS treaty tables: https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/tax-treaty-tables; Ireland Revenue treaty-rate table: https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tax-agreements/rates/index.aspx; Vanguard `VWRA` product page/factsheet sources above.
- `WRLD11`: The Brazilian fund buys `VT`. The same `VT` distribution/withholding mechanics occur inside the Brazilian fund wrapper; Investo's materials note WRLD11's adjusted NAV corrects for dividend withholding on received dividends. The Brazilian individual is then taxed on sale gains in the B3 ETF quota rather than on each underlying dividend receipt. Because the U.S. withholding is suffered inside the Brazilian fund, it is not a foreign tax paid personally by the quota holder and should not be assumed to generate a personal Brazilian credit. Economically, this can layer the embedded U.S. dividend drag and later 15% Brazilian capital-gain tax.
- `VWRA11`: The Brazilian fund buys accumulating `VWRA`. The Irish UCITS dividend withholding layer is embedded inside `VWRA`; the Brazilian fund quota holder is taxed on B3 sale gains. This combines the Ireland dividend layer with local B3 reporting, broadly mirroring direct `VWRA`'s tax layers but with the extra Brazilian wrapper fee.

Primary-source caveat: the exact recoverability of withholding suffered by a fund, and the after-tax treatment of all source-country dividend withholding, is governed by each fund's tax position and each source country's rules. The official sources confirm the broad U.S. 30% NRA rule, the Ireland UCITS domicile, and the U.S.-Ireland 5%/15% dividend treaty-rate framework.

## Ranking for a decades/FIRE investor

This ranking weights tax drag, estate-tax risk, simplicity, and fees. It assumes a buy-and-hold Brazilian tax resident individual who does not need current cash dividends.

1. **Direct `VWRA`**: Best overall if the investor is comfortable with foreign brokerage, FX remittance records, and annual Brazilian reporting under Lei 14.754. It has low current OCF (0.14%), accumulating tax timing, and avoids direct U.S.-situs estate-tax exposure.
2. **`VWRA11`**: Best local-B3 convenience version of the Ireland UCITS idea. The trade-off is the added Brazilian wrapper fee and the current fee-disclosure mismatch: Investo says 0.49%, while Vanguard's current underlying fee implies 0.44% look-through if passed through.
3. **`WRLD11`**: Operationally easy and avoids the investor personally holding U.S.-situs `VT`, but all-in fee is higher than direct `VT` and the U.S. dividend withholding layer is less efficient than the Ireland UCITS layer.
4. **Direct `VT`**: Lowest headline expense ratio, broadest holdings, and very liquid, but usually least attractive for a Brazilian long-horizon investor because of 30% U.S. dividend withholding, recurrent dividend tax-credit paperwork, and direct U.S. estate-tax exposure above the USD 60,000 U.S.-situated asset threshold.
